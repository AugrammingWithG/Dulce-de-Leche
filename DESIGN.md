# Design System: Dulce de Leche Cafe

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Lit Confitería Counter"**

The whole site is a Montevideo confitería at 5am: night-dark chocolate wood, brass trim, lit glass, and cream card stock. The cafe's own treats sit on real shelves in a 3D display case, and every other surface borrows from that counter's furniture: tent-card price labels on brass shelf rails, arched cake niches, a scalloped order ticket, a cake box tied in caramel ribbon. Nothing is a generic "card grid"; each container is a named object from the shop.

Sections alternate between the dark counter (night, wood, chocolate) and lit paper (cream, paper), so scrolling reads as moving between the case and the tissue-paper side of the counter. Density is generous: 120px section padding, a 1180px column, and large Gloock headlines with a Yesteryear script accent that echoes the gold-leaf lettering on the case glass.

Motion follows one grammar, **lift and settle**: things rise out of place on reveal or hover and settle back on a long, soft deceleration (`cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)`). The 3D hero, the studio renders, the cake builder and the CSS all use it.

Behind the counter, the same world runs in an **Operate register**: the staff "counter view" (`admin.html`). It keeps the wood, the card-stock shelves on brass rails, caramel for action and brass for trim, but trades the showroom's air for working density: a 64px wood bar, shelves of hairline-divided stock cells, and one orders rail by pickup time. No 3D, no reveals, no script beyond the wordmark. Its one move is the counter ticking: when an order lands, the changed cells flash brass and roll their numbers, and the order slides into the rail.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Dark wood and lit cream surfaces alternate by section; brass is the trim, caramel is the action.
- Every container is a shop object: tent card, brass rail, arch niche, ticket, cake box.
- Real 3D treats (Three.js) are the imagery: the hero case, the menu cards and the arches all render the same treat builders.
- One motion grammar, lift and settle, on one easing curve.
- Honest placeholders: anything not supplied by the cafe is labelled as such.
- Two registers, one world: the customer site is the showroom; the counter view is the same counter at working density, sharing one store with the site.

## Colors

A warm, low-key night palette of chocolate woods and cream stock, lit by brass and one caramel action colour, with Uruguay's sky blue kept out of the interface entirely.

### Primary
- **Dulce de Leche Caramel** (caramel): the action colour. Primary buttons ("Order a cake", "Next", "Pay and place order", "New order", "Save order"), the counter view's one next-step button per order, the current builder step bar, switch-on state, text selection, the scrollbar thumb, cake-booking bars on the dashboard calendar, the full-width ticker band, and in the counter view, today's sales bars. Hover lifts to **Toasted Caramel** (caramel-hi), which also colours the script "5am" in the H1 and the status dot of a new, requested or enquiry order. **Burnt Caramel** (caramel-deep) is the text-weight version on cream: "Pre-order" links on tent cards, confirmation text, tel links in the order detail, the lower edge of the brass rail gradient.

### Secondary
- **Case Brass** (brass): the material of the display case trim, the cloche rim and knob, the Sun of May, and the dashboard tag tint. In the counter view it is the day before: the yesterday bar (55%) beside today's caramel, the order-type tag tint (16%) and the selected-segment and selected-tab tint (22%).
- **Polished Brass** (brass-hi): gilded lettering and trim on dark grounds: the script wordmark, shelf labels, nav underline, focus ring, dashboard times, order-rail pickup times and group labels, the text of selected segments and tabs, the "Sample data · Reset" chip, the "Sold out today" tag on the site, the status dot of an order in progress, and the top of the shelf-rail gradient. **Brass Flash** (flash) is its lit-paper form: the background a stock cell flashes when its count changes.

### Tertiary
- **Uruguay Sky** (sky): reserved. The opening-day pennant that used it was removed at the client's request (15 Oct 2026), so it currently appears in no interface chrome.
- **Working states** (counter view only, always on card stock or cream): **Oxblood** (danger) for error text, invalid-field borders, the "fewer" delta and the Cancel order ring (danger-line), which warms to **Oxblood Wash** (danger-bg) on hover; **Herb Green** (success) for the "more" delta and **Sage Dot** (success-dot) for a ready order's status dot on wood; **Burnt Toffee** (warn) on **Toffee Wash** (warn-bg) for the Running low flag.

### Neutral
- **5am Night** (night): page ground, hero, dashboard section, the counter view's page and its rail on phones, text on caramel and sky.

- **Chocolate** (choc): order-ahead section, ink button, selected chips, slots, calendar days, completed step bars, booked note; in the counter view the Sold out flag, the selected segment on card stock, the current status step, and the toast. **Milk Chocolate** (choc-2) is its hover.

- **Cocoa Ink** (ink) and **Soft Cocoa Ink** (ink-soft): text on light grounds; soft ink carries descriptions, secondary labels and placeholder notes.
- **Steamed Milk** (on-dark) and **Latte Foam** (on-dark-soft): text on dark grounds; the soft tone carries ledes and captions.
- **Paper Rule** (line), **Pressed Edge** (line-strong) and **Brass Haze** (line-dark): hairlines on light, control borders on light, and hairlines on dark.

### Named Rules
**The One Action Rule.** Caramel means "do this" or "this is on". The exceptions are the ticker band and, in the counter view, today's sales bars, where caramel is today against brass for the day before. No other hue is ever an action-button fill.

**The Opening-Day Sky Rule.** Uruguay sky blue is a celebration colour, not a palette colour, and it currently appears nowhere in the interface (the grand-opening pennant was removed). Frosting colours inside 3D cake renders are product content, not chrome, and are exempt.

**The Lit Stock Rule.** Light surfaces are never pure white. They are cream, paper or card stock, so they read as lit paper against the wood.

**The Brass Selection Rule.** Selection is never caramel. On wood a selected segment or tab takes a brass tint (22% case brass, a 50% polished-brass ring and polished-brass text); on card stock it fills chocolate. Caramel stays free to mean the action.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Gloock (with Times New Roman, serif)
**Body Font:** Figtree (with system-ui, sans-serif)
**Script Font:** Yesteryear (with cursive)

**Character:** Gloock is a high-contrast, sign-painted serif that reads like confitería shop lettering. Figtree keeps the working text plain and friendly. Yesteryear is the gold-leaf script on the glass, used for the wordmark and a few warm words.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (Gloock 400, clamp(2.3rem, 4.4vw, 4.5rem), 1.0, -0.02em): the hero H1 only, max 15ch, with a soft dark text shadow over the 3D case. 2.2rem at 760px and below.
- **Headline** (Gloock 400, clamp(2.1rem, 4vw, 3.4rem), 1.04): section H2s, balanced wrapping.
- **Subhead** (Gloock 400, 1.6 to 2rem, about 1.1): builder step headings, the ticket and form titles, visit details, story quote (clamp(1.7rem, 3vw, 2.5rem), 1.3).
- **Title** (Gloock 400, 1.15 to 1.3rem, 1.15): tent-card names, arch names, order-list item names, shelf labels (1.5rem, polished brass), prices on tent cards.
- **Body** (Figtree 400, 17px, 1.6; 16px at 560px and below): ledes capped at 56 to 60ch, descriptions at .86 to .92rem.
- **Label** (Figtree 600 to 700, .78 to .9rem): field labels, step names, tags, dashboard headings, button text (.98rem, 700). Sentence case throughout.
- **Script** (Yesteryear 400): the wordmark (2rem in the nav, 2.6rem in the footer, 1.7rem in the counter view), the giant ghosted "Dulce de Leche" behind the hero (14% brass), the hero's "5am", the hand-written hero note, the gilded sign and the box label in 3D.

### Operate register (the counter view)
- **Date title** (Gloock 400, 2rem, 1.1, -0.01em; 1.6rem at 560px): the day being viewed, "Friday 16 October".
- **Shelf and rail titles** (Gloock 400, 1.5rem): shelf names in polished brass; "Orders" in steamed milk.
- **Drawer title** (Gloock 400, 1.6rem): "New order", the customer's name in the order detail.
- **UI** (Figtree 400, 16px, 1.5, tabular figures on the body): everything else, all data included.
- **Count** (Figtree 700, 2rem, 1.1; 1.6rem at 560px and for untracked items): the number left in a stock cell.
- **UI button and small** (Figtree 700, .95rem; .82rem for next-step, "+1 sold" and segment labels): sentence case.
- **Caption sizes** (.78 to .86rem): item name over flavour (.8rem soft ink over .92rem bold), bar labels, deltas, status lines, tags and flags.

### Named Rules
**The Gold-Leaf Rule.** Script is lettering on glass, not a body face. Use it for the name and one or two warm words per view, never for a sentence of instructions.

**The Sentence Case Rule.** No uppercase tracked labels in Figtree. The only spaced capitals are the "CAFE" wordmark suffix in Gloock (.32em), which comes from the cafe's own logo.

**The Counter Register Rule.** In an Operate surface Figtree carries all UI and data, with tabular figures throughout. Gloock appears only as the date, shelf, rail and drawer titles; Yesteryear only as the wordmark.

## Layout

A single centred column, `min(1180px, 100% - 48px)` (100% - 32px at 560px and below). Section bands run full bleed with 120px vertical padding (110 to 130px). Section heads are a two-column grid (title and lede left, placeholder note right) that stacks at 560px.

The hero is a 220vh scroll track with a 100vh sticky stage; scroll progress drives the 3D camera and the overlay copy through a `--p` custom property. Content grids are asymmetric pairs (1.15fr / .85fr, 1.05fr / .95fr, .9fr / 1.1fr) with 40 to 64px gaps; the menu is a wrapping flex row of tent cards on each shelf with an 18px gap; occasions are four arches, two at 980px and below.

Breakpoints: 980px (nav collapses to a Menu toggle, all pair grids stack, sticky panels unstick, dashboard sidebar hides), 760px (hero copy stacks, hero note hides), 560px (body 16px, narrower gutter, steps nav 3 across, footer single column).

Spacing rhythm in practice: 4, 8, 12, 18, 24, 48, 64 and 120px.

### The counter view
Full width, no centred column. A sticky 64px wood top bar over a two-column grid: the shelves on the left (24px padding, 30px between shelves) and a 400px orders rail on the right, sticky under the bar and scrolling on its own. At 1100px the rail narrows to 340px. At 900px the grid becomes one column behind Stock and Orders tabs (sticky under the bar), search moves into the Orders view, and a fixed wood New order bar takes the bottom of the screen. At 560px the wordmark loses "Counter", the sample chip moves inline under the date, cells go two across, and the bar labels stack. Shelf cells fill `minmax(190px, 1fr)` columns, so a whole shelf reads in one or two rows on a laptop. Spacing runs tighter here: 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24px. Every press target is at least 36px tall; primary controls and tabs are 44px, the phone bar 48px.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is lit, not stacked. Shadows are soft, long and pulled in (large negative spread), like objects sitting under the case lights on a counter. Nothing floats on a hard edge. On the 3D side, depth comes from real lighting: ACES filmic tone mapping, a RoomEnvironment reflection map, warm point lights inside the case, and soft shadow maps.

### Named Rules
**The Case Light Rule.** Every shadow falls straight down, soft and tucked under its object. No offset, no hard edge, no coloured glow.

## Shapes

Pills for anything you press (buttons, chips, slots, qty steppers, fact tags, switches). Objects from the shop keep their real silhouettes: tent cards have rounded tops and nearly square feet (8px 8px 3px 3px) and stand tipped back 5 degrees in perspective; arches are full-round tops on 14px feet; the ticket has a 6px top and a scalloped tear-off bottom edge (CSS radial-gradient mask, 9px scallops every 22px). Panels use 12px (inputs), 18px (form, dashboard) and 22px (3D stage, map). Dividers are 1px hairlines, dashed on paper goods (tent top perforation, ticket lines).

In the counter view the stock shelf is a wide tent: rounded 12px top corners on 3px feet, standing on a 6px brass rail that overhangs it by 10px each side. Order rows, inputs and item tiles use 12px; the inline made input 8px. Segments, tabs, flags, tags, status steps, the sale and next-step buttons, the toast and the sample chip are pills; status marks are 8px dots. The sample chip alone has a dashed Brass Haze border, the mark of something not real.

### The 3D system (Three.js 0.169)
- **Shared renderer setup:** ACES filmic tone mapping (exposure 1.05), sRGB output, RoomEnvironment PMREM reflections, warm hemisphere and key lights, pixel ratio capped at 1.75 (1.5 on small screens), shadows off on small screens, render loops paused off screen.
- **Materials:** brass (metalness 1, roughness .24), clear glass (physical, opacity .09, no depth write), porcelain (physical, clearcoat .6), lace doilies from a generated alpha texture. All food uses `food()`: a physical material painted from tileable noise layers (blotches, mottling, grain, speckles, pores, spatula streaks) with a matching bump map. Gloss is reserved for glazes and dulce de leche (clearcoat up to 1, dulce #7A3D15). Flour, sugar paste and frosting get a powdery sheen. Shapes get hand-made irregularity from `organic()` noise displacement. Realism details include tierGeo rounded frosting edges, teardrop drips under an overhanging pour, drupelet raspberries, seeded strawberries, piped star rosettes, flaky puff sheets, and icing-sugar dusting via instanced flecks.
- **Treat builders:** one shared set of procedural models: croissant (filled), alfajor (coconut or chocolate), muffin (Kit Kat, berry, peach), milhoja, cheesecake, Biscoff pastry, Continental slice, chivito, coffee cup with latte art, and `buildCake` (tiers, frosting, drip, berries or sugar flowers, gilded script topper). The hero, studio renders and builder all draw from these, so a treat looks the same everywhere.
- **Display-case hero:** a wood and brass case with glass shelves on two decks either side of a tall centre bay, so the glass cloche over the celebration cake can rise straight up. At load a 4:59 to 5:00 clock plays and the case lights flicker on. Scroll eases the camera in to the cake (cubic ease-out) while the cloche lifts 1.22 units and the front glass fades. Hovering a treat lifts it, turns it slowly and shows a floating tent card; clicking adds it to the order or jumps to the builder. Sugar dust drifts and the coffee on top steams.
- **Studio renderer (`studio()`):** one offscreen renderer with a shadow-catcher floor that photographs treat builders at a set elevation and azimuth, trims the image to its rendered pixels (shadow included) and hands back a PNG for tent cards (760×480) and arches (640×760, on a porcelain stand). ### The tick (signature move)
The counter view's one move. When a count changes, from a website order arriving, a sale or an edit, each changed cell flashes Brass Flash and fades back over 1.4s while its number rolls down into place (60% drop, .7s). ### The shared store
The customer site and the counter view read and write one browser store (`ddl-store-v1`), and each listens for the other's changes. Website pre-orders and cake bookings land in the rail as "Website" orders. Made counts, counter sales and live pickup orders give today's number left per flavour; at zero the site shows the item sold out. Cancelling an order puts its stock back. Cakes and catering never draw on shelf stock. This is the mockup's stand-in for the production backend; the visual contract (orders flow in, stock marks sold out) stays when it moves.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** make every container a shop object: tent card on a brass rail, arch niche, ticket, box. Name new components from the counter before reaching for a plain card.
- **Do** use caramel (#C8894B) for the one primary action per view and for "on" states; use chocolate fills for selected choices.
- **Do** move things with lift and settle: rise 2px (buttons, chips), 6px (arches, renders) or 8px (tent cards), and reveal content with a 26px rise, 6px blur and fade over .9 to 1.1s, all on `cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)`.
- **Do** reveal section headlines with the lift-title clip (18px rise behind a clip-path wipe, 1.1s).
- **Do** render product imagery with the shared 3D treat builders and `studio()` until the cafe's real photos arrive, then place photos inside the same frames (tent art, arches).
- **Do** label anything the cafe has not supplied as a placeholder: "From your menu", "Your photos go here", "(sample data)", "Demo: no payment is taken".
- **Do** honour `prefers-reduced-motion`: no smooth scroll, no ticker, no reveal transitions, no view transitions, no intro clock, no idle spins or drops. Keep `?static` working: hero fixed at 900px, everything revealed, camera at rest, for clean captures.
- **Do** use tabular numerals for times, quantities and calendar days.
- **Do** run staff surfaces in the Operate register: Figtree with tabular figures for all UI and data, Gloock only for date, shelf, rail and drawer titles, 44px primary targets and 36px minimum.
- **Do** pair today in caramel with the day before in brass wherever the counter view compares days.
- **Do** give every live order exactly one caramel next-step button, and put destructive actions behind an inline confirm.
- **Do** keep sample data labelled, resettable and dated around today, with invented names and fictitious numbers.

### Don't:
- **Don't** invent prices, ratings, testimonials, awards or reviews. Only the chivito ($20) and chips ($3) have real prices today.
- **Don't** use Uruguay sky blue in the interface chrome.
- **Don't** fill an action button with any colour other than caramel, chocolate, or transparent with a ring. Selected toggles on wood take the brass tint, card-stock controls stay on their paper, and only the destructive Cancel order ring warms to oxblood wash on hover.
- **Don't** use pure white surfaces or pure black text; use card stock and cocoa ink.
- **Don't** use hard offset shadows or coloured glows; shadows fall straight down under the case light.
- **Don't** set sentences in Yesteryear or add uppercase tracked labels in Figtree.
- **Don't** introduce a second easing curve for UI motion; the house curve is `cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)`.
- **Don't** mark selection in caramel; on wood it is a brass tint, on card stock chocolate.
- **Don't** use the state colours (oxblood, herb green, toffee) on the customer site or as fills for actions; they belong to working states on the counter view.
